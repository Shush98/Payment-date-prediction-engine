Expected Value Framework
EV = P(late) × P(responds) × 3 days × 0.03%/day × Invoice Amount − $15 call cost
P(late) — Probability of Late Payment
Derived from the prediction interval position. If the upper bound of the 80% PI is well above zero, P(late) is high. If even the pessimistic scenario predicts on-time payment, P(late) approaches zero. This uses uncertainty, not just the point prediction.
P(responds) — Customer Responsiveness
Inverse of payment variability. Customers with consistent payment patterns (low std dev) tend to respond to collection calls. Erratic payers (high std dev) are less predictable and score lower on responsiveness, clipped to [0.2, 0.8].
Causal Insights
📅
Customer History Dominates
Past behavior explains 79.8% of prediction variance. A customer's average days late is by far the strongest signal — better than any invoice-level feature.
GBM feature importance (mean model): cust_avg_days_late = 65.8%
📆
Year-End Accounting Pressure
December invoices are paid ~2 days earlier on average, driven by customers closing their own books. This is a natural experiment — exogenous timing pressure reduces payment delay.
is_year_end feature; seasonal pattern consistent across 2018–2020
📄
Payment Terms as Structural Constraint
Payment terms (net-30, net-60, etc.) explain 8.1% of variance — more than all temporal features combined. Contractual structure shapes behavior more than invoice timing.
payment_terms_encoded = 8.1% importance; 4th highest feature
